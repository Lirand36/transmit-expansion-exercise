Problem

150 of 900 marketing leads have a company name with a typo or a different ending, so they can't be tied to an account.

An untied lead is invisible in the plan. If a customer asks for a demo under a misspelled name, we miss the signal.

AI advantage

The AI reads a name the way a person does, for all 150 names in seconds.

It knows a typo of our customer from a different company with a similar name, and it says “no match” when it isn't sure.

How it works

  1. Code tries first. 750 of 900 leads match an account name exactly. Done.
  2. Code shortlists. For each of the other 150, it picks the 5 most similar account names.
  3. The AI picks. It chooses one of the 5, or says “no match”. We ask it twice, separately.
  4. Same answer twice, and sure: the lead is tied to that account.
  5. Different answers, or not sure: the lead waits in a review table until a person says yes or no.

Honest accuracy

How we measured. We wrote down the right answer for each of the 150 names, one by one: which account it belongs to, or “not a customer”. 69 belong to our accounts. Then we compared the AI's answers with that list.

How would you know it's wrong?

Ask twice

We ask the same question twice, separately. When the AI is sure, it gives the same answer both times. When it's guessing, the answers differ, and a person decides. Today the answers match on 149 of 150 names. If that number drops, something changed, such as a new model or a new kind of name.

Check against the reps

When a rep converts a lead in the CRM, the rep picks the account by hand. If the rep's account differs from the AI's, the lead goes to the review table. Over time, the share that agrees shows the AI's real accuracy.

What happens when it is wrong?

Unsure means “no match”

The worst case is a missed lead, never a lead tied to the wrong account.

A person decides, and the plan waits

Unsure leads go to a review table with a daily alert. No new priority list until the table is empty. Each decision is saved and reused.

If the AI is down

It tries the next model. If all are down, only new messy names wait. Old answers are saved, so the plan still builds. After 2 working days down, new names go to the review table for a person. If a model is retired (this happened during the build), we switch to another one and re-run the accuracy test before using it.

Where we chose not to use AI

Anywhere a number becomes money

The $ value of the plan, expected bookings, win rate and currency conversion. A CRO commits to these numbers, so they must come out the same every time. Plain code does that.

The safety checks on the data

The rules that stop a run when data is broken, such as a deal with no account. They must be exact yes-or-no rules.

Built today vs. explained

BuiltAsk twice. The review table that stops a new priority list until a person decides. Saved decisions, reused every month. Model fallback. Off switch. The accuracy test against the answer list.
ExplainedThe daily alert on the review table, the check against the reps' picks, and the 2-day rule when the AI is down. All three need the live CRM and the automation platform.

What we refused to answer

How much pipeline do we have today?

Not answerable: the CRM export has no open deals. We answer how much we could create in Q1 ($1.6M on 24 accounts) instead.

Are the 19 customers with no contract on record (12 + 7) using us for free?

We can't tell a free user from a deal missing in the CRM. Both are money, so we flag them and don't claim lost revenue.

Which accounts will expand on their own?

Nothing in the data predicts it better than chance. We tested usage, leads, demo requests, segment and timing against past expansion deals. So the plan prioritizes on facts and sales logic, and Q1 is the test.

Why do existing customers ask for a demo?

The leads file has no job title or product of interest. The plan names the person who asked, so the rep can find out.

  • It counted a conference lead the same as a demo request. Now demo requests count most and event touches least.

  • Its first monitoring answers described processes we never agreed on. Rewritten around what we can actually run.
