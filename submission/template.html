How we'd know it's wrong, 3 months on

  • Ask twice. The two answers agree on 149 of 150 today. A drop means something changed.
  • Reps' picks. When a rep's account differs from the AI's, the lead goes to review.

What happens when it's wrong

  • Unsure = no match. Worst case is a missed lead, never a wrong customer.
  • A person decides. No new plan until they do.
  • AI unavailable. 3 backup models are tried in order. If all fail, those leads stay unmatched and retry next run. The plan still builds from saved answers.
  • Off switch. One setting turns the AI off. Messy names then simply stay out of the plan.

Where there's no LLM

  • Anywhere a number becomes money: the plan's $, win rates, currency.
  • Data safety checks: exact yes-or-no rules.

What it changes

Leads land on the right customer, so marketing signals reach the plan. This quarter, 1 demo request on the plan came only through the AI (Cascad Energy).

Running today

How I spent the 4 hours

75–80% thinking. 20–25% building.

Before writing any code, I sat with the question and the data. What does the data show, and what doesn't it? Which assumptions are hidden in the question? Then I shaped the answer for the person asking: a CRO, who needs three things.

  • Business logic they can trust
  • Where the revenue is
  • Decisions only a leader makes: retention, sales effort, and data fixes

My “CRO Reviewer” tested every step against these three. A strong base made the build faster and more accurate, with fewer rounds. What I'd do next is listed below.

Tools
  • Claude Code: the builder. Built the pipeline and the data model, cleaned and joined the four files, and wrote the code, the plan page and this submission, one approved step at a time.
  • Gemini: the AI inside the pipeline. Powers the lead-matching component: 148 of 150 messy names decided correctly.
  • ChatGPT: “CRO Reviewer”, my QA agent. A devil's advocate I set up to review every version as the CRO would, for business, revenue and leadership sense. It kept the plan aimed at the main stakeholder.
What I delegated

The code, the pages, first drafts of the text, and a first draft of the answer list that measures the AI.

What I checked myself
  • Every assumption needed my OK before it was used. Business rules the data can't tell, like “outbound is never MQL'd”, came from me.
  • I asked why customers who already use the product request a demo. That surfaced 7 accounts still using us after a lost renewal.
  • I was the person in the loop: I decided the one lead the AI wasn't sure about.
  • I read every line that gets presented, looking for made-up claims and wording a CRO wouldn't follow.
What the AI got wrong that I caught
  • It read blank MQL dates on outbound leads as missing data. The blank is correct. The error is the 59 outbound leads that do have one.
  • It scored every marketing touch. The data didn't support it (leads alone predict expansion at 0.52, a coin flip is 0.5), so only demo requests are shown, as a signal, not scored.
  • It kept building plan pages full of numbers, words and targets the data didn't support. I pushed it to one headline and five plays, with details on click.
  • It offered a statistical forecast (~$54k of pipeline) as the answer. I had it tested: barely better than a coin flip (0.60). So the plan is built on plays instead.
  • It claimed the plan would be pushed into the CRM as tasks. Nothing like that was built, so I had it removed.
  • Its first monitoring answers described processes we never agreed on. Rewritten around what we can actually run.
What I'd do next
  • Pull open deals and renewal dates from the CRM. Then Q1 pipeline coverage and the Q1 expansion window become real numbers.
  • Pull loss reasons for the 13 lost renewals ($1.9M), to know what to fix.
  • Run the plan monthly. Each quarter, check whether last quarter's plays actually expanded.
  • Connect the AI review table's alert and the reps' check to the live CRM. These are explained today, not built.
